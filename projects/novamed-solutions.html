NovaMed Solutions Performance Analysis

A performance analytics initiative for a pharmaceutical company, tracking sales trends, profitability and customer behaviour to guide product, customer and market decisions.

Tool: Power BI Data: Sales, COGS, Customers, Products, Regions

Background

NovaMed Solutions is a pharmaceutical company that develops, manufactures and distributes prescription and over-the-counter drugs, with core operations across North America, Europe and parts of Asia-Pacific. Facing increasing competition and shifting market demands, this analytics initiative was built to give visibility into high-performing products, underperforming segments, and margin-optimisation opportunities — supporting decisions in product strategy, customer management, market expansion, and operational efficiency (COGS control).

Sales Performance Dashboard

Sales trend

Revenue and profit start strong in January, then dip in February — a demand-driven dip, since COGS stays steady, pointing to consistent production and supply cost rather than a cost problem. Recovery begins by March, with sustained growth through April to June. July is the best-performing month of the year on both revenue and profit, before a moderate, proportionate decline through August and September.

Top and bottom profit contributors (customers)

Bob is the highest-profit customer at $12.5M, likely a high-volume or premium buyer worth retaining through loyalty incentives. John ($11.48M) and David ($11.55M) follow closely with stable, well-balanced margins. Together, the top three customers contribute roughly 50% or more of total profit — a healthy top tier, but also a concentration risk. At the other end, Alice ($8.86M), Jane ($7.75M) and Carol ($6.22M) trail well behind, a gap of about $6M that points to pricing, discounting or service-cost issues worth investigating.

Top and bottom drug performers

Lisinopril leads on profitability with a strong balance of profit, quantity and revenue share, followed by the consistently reliable Albuterol. Warfarin is a standout anomaly: 82.29% profit concentration against a low sales volume — a niche, high-margin product with real revenue-dependency risk if demand ever declines. Ciprofloxacin and Omeprazole show high cost-to-profit ratios, suggesting thin margins or over-discounting worth reviewing.

Customer Analysis Dashboard

Revenue by region

North America dominates total revenue generation, with Australia contributing a moderate share and Asia and South America barely registering. That's a real concentration risk — any regulatory, economic or supply disruption in North America would materially hit overall performance, and it signals an underdeveloped sales presence elsewhere.

Buyer type

Sellers drive the large majority of revenue over direct users — in Canada alone, sellers generated $28.11M against $3.55M from users. That's a strong B2B-driven model, with room to grow through direct-to-consumer channels for margin expansion.

Age group and gender

Adults generate the highest average revenue per customer ($23.67M), followed by middle-aged customers ($20.95M); youth ($16.81M) and older ($10M) segments underperform, likely due to differences in income stability and healthcare consumption. By gender, male customers lead purchase quantity (46.81%) over female customers (32.41%), leaving room for gender-specific campaigns.

Recommendations

  1. Strengthen relationships and tailored contracts with the top customers (Bob, David, John); evaluate pricing and incentive structures for the lowest contributors (Alice, Jane, Carol).
  2. Maintain focus on the top-margin drugs (Lisinopril, Albuterol, Gabapentin); reassess Warfarin's pricing and production given its extreme profit-to-volume imbalance, and review procurement costs for Ciprofloxacin and Omeprazole.
  3. Plan for seasonal demand — increase production and marketing across May to July, and schedule maintenance or cost-control work during the slower February and September periods.
  4. Audit supply-chain costs to lower COGS on underperforming drugs, and explore alternative suppliers or packaging.
  5. Reduce dependency on the top 3 customers and top 3 drugs by promoting mid-tier products and cross-selling to existing clients.
  6. Expand geographically beyond North America — deepen presence in Germany, the UK and France, and study market entry into Asia-Pacific and Latin America.
  7. Deepen seller partnerships through loyalty incentives while building direct-to-consumer digital channels to capture untapped margin.
  8. Target youth through digital and social channels, and develop specialised offerings (such as chronic care) for older customers.
