Choco de Lax Performance Analysis

An interactive dashboard for a premium chocolate brand, analysing sales across European locations, channels, salespeople and product lines to guide product positioning and channel strategy.

Tool: Tableau Data: Sales, Channels, Salespeople, Products, Locations

Background

Choco de Luxe is a premium artisanal chocolate brand headquartered in Brussels, Belgium, distributing chocolate bars, bites and confections across several European cities through retail outlets, online platforms and supermarkets. As its product line and footprint grew, the company needed a unified, data-driven view of how its products, salespeople and delivery strategies were performing across Europe — to identify patterns, inefficiencies and growth opportunities, and to inform product positioning and channel strategy.

Location Performance

Spain and Italy are the overall top performers, combining high sales volume with strong delivery success (33,446/197 and 34,274/199 respectively) — the clear high-growth markets. The Netherlands sells nearly as much (32,395) but trails on delivery success (193), pointing to a fulfilment problem rather than a demand problem. France and Switzerland show the opposite pattern: strong delivery success but low sales volume, which is worth investigating. Belgium and Germany underperform on both sales volume and delivery success (around 21,400/127 each), likely due to delivery delays or high cancellation rates.

Sales Channels

Website is the strongest channel overall — £1,119,865 in profit from 496 transactions, driving 45% of total sales and 46% of total profit, making it the primary growth engine. Warehouse delivers the best value per transaction (£692,754 across just 299 transactions). Social Media matches Warehouse on transaction count but trails it on profitability, suggesting heavier discounting relative to Warehouse's better per-transaction pricing.

Salesperson Performance

Against a £100,000 profit target per salesperson, the top 5 all deliver volumes of 8,700–9,600 boxes and profits of £118K–£132K. Karlen McCaffrey leads with £131,994 profit on 9,658 boxes shipped, followed by Beverie Moffet, Kelci Walkden, Oby Sorrel and Dennison Crosswaite — all clearing target. The bottom 5 fall well short, with volumes of 4,000–5,600 boxes and profits of £54K–£82K.

Product Profitability

Eclairs are the clear top profit driver at £195.1K, £49.3K ahead of the next product. Dark chocolate variants dominate the rest of the top 10 — 99% Dark & Pure (£145.8K), 85% Dark Bars (£125.5K), 50% Dark Bites (£117.1K) and 70% Dark Bites (£114.3K) — with mid-tier products clustered tightly between £113K and £136K.

Organic vs. Non-organic

Non-organic products dominate every channel. Website drives the highest volume in both categories (69,610 non-organic, 11,183 organic), while Social Media has the weakest organic performance of the three channels.

Recommendations

  1. Scale what's working: keep Website as the primary growth engine — invest in conversion (offers, bundles, loyalty, upsell), improve checkout and retention campaigns, and prioritise stock availability for top sellers.
  2. Improve channel efficiency: expand Warehouse activity using stronger demand generation (bulk deals, distributor push), and improve Social Media margins by reducing heavy discounting and pushing higher-margin bundles.
  3. Focus on high-profit products: protect inventory and prioritise production for Eclairs and the Dark chocolate range; expand the range with new variations and seasonal editions; bundle high-margin items with mid-tier products.
  4. Strengthen organic strategy: grow organic through Warehouse and Website first, since they show the best return, and reposition organic on Social Media around storytelling and premium value rather than discounting.
  5. Salesforce performance: use top performers as a benchmark for product mix and customer strategy; coach bottom performers with better leads and territories, bundle scripts, and weekly target tracking.
